Phased approach

One corridor at a time

A practical program would pick a stretch of road, finish it, and use what it learned on the next stretch. It would not try to bury every line in Walton County in a single project.

  1. Study the corridor

    Map who owns the poles, which lines are electric, and which are cable or phone. Note driveways, drainage, trees, and pavement that is already scheduled for work. This step produces a feasibility study, not a construction crew.

  2. Design with the utilities

    The electric utility engineers the new route. Other companies on the poles say whether they will move too. Easements are identified where a transformer pad or cabinet has to sit.

  3. Build in sections

    Crews install conduit along a few blocks, switch customers to the new service, then move down the road. Through traffic may shift. Local access is the standard expectation, as it has been on the Town of Palm Beach’s phased work.

  4. Remove poles only when they are empty

    A pole stays until every wire on it has a new home. An electric-only project can leave poles standing for cable and phone. That is a common outcome, and it should be said up front.

  5. Choose the next corridor

    After one stretch is done, the county and the utilities can look at cost, outages, and disruption, then decide whether a second corridor is worth it. Stopping after a pilot is an allowed outcome.

Bundle it with work that is already opening the ground

The expensive and annoying part of undergrounding is often the digging and the restoration, not only the cable. If Walton County, a state road project, or a drainage project is already rebuilding a stretch of pavement, sidewalk, or storm pipe, that is a chance to lay conduit in the same opening.

The Town of Palm Beach’s project updates describe this kind of overlap in real time: underground conduit, horizontal drilling, paving, and in some blocks a watermain, scheduled on the same streets. Residents still live with lane closures. They are less likely to live through two separate digs.

Bundling is an idea to put in a feasibility study. It is not a claim that a 30A road project is already funded for this purpose.

What “phased” rules out. It rules out a sudden order to take down every pole. It also rules out a promise that the whole coast will look finished by a certain year. No year is proposed here.
