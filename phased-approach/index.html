Phased approach

One corridor at a time

Study, then a decision. Design, then a decision. Build one section, transfer service and remove poles, then evaluate. Only then would anyone decide on another possible corridor. This is not a plan to bury every line in Walton County at once.

  1. Study

    Map who owns the poles, which lines are electric, and which are cable or phone. Note driveways, drainage, trees, and pavement that is already scheduled for work. This step produces a feasibility study, not a construction crew.

  2. Decision: design, or stop

    The study can end here. Facts may show that a possible corridor is a poor candidate, that ownership is too split, or that the cost is not worth a design.

  3. Design

    If a design is authorized, the utility that owns the poles would engineer a route. On Scenic Highway 30A, that utility is likely CHELCO for essentially the whole corridor. A feasibility study would confirm ownership before a route is treated as settled. Miramar Beach, west of the 30A corridor, is largely FPL and would be its own question. It is not a segment of 30A. Other companies on the poles would say whether they will move too. Easements are identified where a transformer pad or cabinet would have to sit. Design is not a commitment to build.

  4. Decision: build one section, or stop

    A finished design is another stopping point. No section has to be built because a drawing exists.

  5. Build one section

    If construction were approved for one section, crews would install conduit along those blocks and switch customers to the new service. Through traffic may shift. Local access is the usual expectation, as it has been on the Town of Palm Beach’s phased work. One section is not a countywide project.

  6. Transfer service and remove poles

    Service moves to the underground lines. A pole stays until every wire on it has a new home. An electric-only job can leave poles standing for cable and phone. That is a common outcome, and it should be said up front.

  7. Evaluate

    After one section, look at cost, outages, and disruption before talking about any other possible corridor. Stopping after one corridor is an allowed outcome. Scenic Highway 30A runs from its western connection with U.S. 98 near Dune Allen to Inlet Beach. It does not run to Miramar Beach. CHELCO likely serves essentially all of that road, so the next stretch of 30A is not assumed to be a different electric company. A feasibility study would confirm ownership.

  8. Decide on the next corridor

    Another potential study area would be a new decision. Miramar Beach lies west of Scenic Highway 30A. It is not the west end of that road, and it is largely FPL. Nothing in an earlier section requires a later one.

Stopping is an acceptable outcome. A feasibility study can end without design. A design can end without construction. One finished section can be the last. No later corridor is owed.

Bundle it with work that is already opening the ground

The expensive and annoying part of undergrounding is often the digging and the restoration, not only the cable. If Walton County, a state road project, or a drainage project is already rebuilding a stretch of pavement, sidewalk, or storm pipe, that is a chance to lay conduit in the same opening.

The Town of Palm Beach’s 2026 project notes still describe this kind of overlap: underground conduit, horizontal drilling, street restoration, and, on some blocks, a watermain. Residents still live with lane closures. Bundling is meant to avoid a second dig. It does not make the construction quiet.

Bundling is an idea to put in a feasibility study. It is not a claim that a 30A road project is already funded for this purpose.

What “phased” rules out. It rules out a sudden order to take down every pole. It also rules out a promise that the whole coast will look finished by a certain year. No year is proposed here.
