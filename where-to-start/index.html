Where to start

Two potential study areas

Scenic Highway 30A and Miramar Beach are separate suggestions for a feasibility study. Walton County has not adopted either one. No utility has agreed to build anything.

A and B are not the same road

Scenic Highway 30A runs from its western connection with U.S. 98 near Dune Allen to Inlet Beach in the east. Miramar Beach lies west of the 30A corridor and is suggested as a separate coastal area worth studying. It is not the western end of Scenic Highway 30A.

A. Scenic Highway 30A

A possible corridor from the U.S. 98 connection near Dune Allen east through the 30A communities to Inlet Beach. A feasibility study could ask which overhead segments are realistic to consider, who owns them, and what the work would take. Suggesting the corridor is not selecting it.

B. Miramar Beach

A separate coastal area west of Scenic Highway 30A, toward Destin. It is its own potential study area, not a segment of 30A. The same study questions would apply: ownership, overhead versus underground, constraints, and cost. It is not an approved project.

The reasons to look at either place are ordinary ones. The coast is scenic and heavily used. Storms come off the Gulf. Trees and poles stand close to the pavement in many blocks. That does not prove these are the cheapest miles in the county. A feasibility study would test that.

The schematic is orientation only, not a survey.

How should a corridor be evaluated?

These are factors a feasibility study could examine. They are not a ranking, and they are not a decision to build.

Evacuation routes

A line that falls across a road people need in a storm is a public problem even if the neighborhood behind it is small.

Other scenic corridors

30A is the obvious scenic road. It may not be the only one worth a later phase.

How many people a line serves

A short span that feeds many homes can matter more than a long rural span. Florida utilities already use outage history and customer impact when they pick neighborhood projects.

Repeated outages

Lines with a record of vegetation or storm outages are the ones utilities in Florida have targeted first. A Walton study should ask the utilities for that history rather than guess from memory.

Road work already on the calendar

A repaving, drainage, or sidewalk project can be the practical moment to lay conduit, if the timing lines up. See bundling.

Who owns the pole

A criterion that comes before the others. A corridor can look like one road and still be served by different electric utilities from one end to the other.

More than one electric utility serves this county

Electric service is split. Miramar Beach, west of the 30A corridor, and the Inlet Beach area, at the eastern end of Scenic Highway 30A, are largely Florida Power & Light’s Northwest Florida territory, formerly Gulf Power. Destin, just west of the county line, is in that same FPL territory. Gulf Power merged into FPL effective January 1, 2021, and FPL has served that territory under the FPL name since the start of 2022. Choctawhatchee Electric Cooperative (CHELCO), a member-owned cooperative, says it serves most of Walton County, including much of Santa Rosa Beach, Freeport, and inland Walton. Scenic Highway 30A is not one utility from Dune Allen to Inlet Beach.

FPL’s undergrounding programs apply only where FPL owns the lines, and only under rules set by the Florida Public Service Commission. They do not cover CHELCO’s lines, and they do not cover all of Scenic Highway 30A. This site does not describe a CHELCO undergrounding program, and neither company has a project listed here. This site does not have a block-by-block ownership map. A feasibility study would have to build one. Until then, the name on a power bill is the check for a single address.

Not a construction map. Naming these potential study areas is not a list of streets scheduled for work, and it is not a statement that every pole there should come down.
