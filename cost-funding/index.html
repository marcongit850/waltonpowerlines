Cost and funding

Who might pay, once someone prices a real corridor

Every option below is a concept a study could examine. None has been chosen. This page does not state a Walton County budget, a tax rate, or a cost per mile.

There is no Walton County price yet.

A credible cost needs answers, in order:

  1. Which possible corridor?
  2. Who owns the lines?
  3. What work would be included?
  4. Which funding options could apply to that owner?
  5. Estimated cost

Until those questions are answered, a simple cost-per-mile estimate for Walton County would largely be speculation.

Soil, driveway restorations, transformers, and whether cable and phone move at the same time all change the number.

Older statewide numbers show the scale of “do everything,” and they are easy to misuse. In March 2005 the Florida Public Service Commission’s preliminary analysis estimated $94.5 billion to place the existing overhead distribution lines and feeders of Florida’s five investor-owned utilities underground. The estimate largely updated a 1991 study for inflation. A June 2024 Department of Energy case study recounts that figure as a reason Florida did not order a full conversion. It covers investor-owned utilities, not cooperatives such as CHELCO. It is not a bid for 30A, and it is not in today’s dollars.

In California, the public utilities commission has published comparisons in which conversion costs several times more than building a new overhead line, with a wide range. Those are California figures. They are a caution, not a Walton estimate.

Funding options to study later

Grouped here so the choices are easier to tell apart. None is selected. FPL is not CHELCO, and a rule for one does not bind the other.

Utility options

FPL’s storm program, where FPL owns the lines

These rules apply only where FPL owns the lines. In Walton County that is largely Miramar Beach, west of Scenic Highway 30A. Nearby Destin is in the same former Gulf Power territory. CHELCO likely serves essentially all of Scenic Highway 30A, so FPL’s storm-program charges are not the default for that corridor.

Investor-owned utilities file a storm protection plan at least every three years. If the Public Service Commission approves undergrounding inside that plan, the utility may recover prudent costs from its customers generally, not only from the street that was converted. That is how FPL describes its Storm Secure Underground Program: no upfront bill to a neighborhood FPL selects, and a storm-protection charge on FPL bills that also covers other storm work. FPL has not selected a Walton neighborhood on this site.

A conversion the utility did not choose

FPL says a community that asks for a conversion FPL did not select pays the extra cost itself. State rule 25-6.115 sets how that applicant charge is calculated for investor-owned utilities. That example applies on FPL lines, such as at Miramar Beach. It is not the default for Scenic Highway 30A, and it is not a countywide rule.

CHELCO is a different utility

CHELCO, a member-owned cooperative, says it serves most of Walton County, including much of Santa Rosa Beach, Freeport, and inland Walton. CHELCO likely serves essentially all of Scenic Highway 30A. FPL’s storm-plan rules do not apply on CHELCO lines. This site does not describe a CHELCO undergrounding program, and it does not say either utility has agreed to build one here. A feasibility study would confirm ownership.

Local options

County funds

The county commission could choose to pay for a feasibility study, and later for a share of construction, out of its ordinary budget. That would be a public decision made in the open. This site is not that decision.

Assessments

Florida local governments can use a special assessment when a defined set of properties benefits. Whether undergrounding on a given road would qualify, and what the charge would be, is a legal and engineering question. It is not proposed here as a rate.

An MSTU or an MSBU

Florida counties may create a municipal service taxing unit or a municipal service benefit unit for part of the unincorporated area, under section 125.01, Florida Statutes. In plain terms, an MSTU is the version funded by property tax inside a mapped area. An MSBU is the version funded by service charges or assessments. The state’s demographic research office describes that distinction. Either tool would be a county ordinance, with its own hearing. Naming the tool is not creating the district.

Project-specific options

Grants

Cities sometimes seek federal hazard-mitigation money for a portion of resilience work. A grant is not cash until an agency awards it. A study could ask what, if anything, is eligible. This site does not claim an award is available.

Developers, on new projects

New subdivisions are a different case from converting old lines. Investor-owned utilities file a tariff for underground service in new subdivisions. The applicant pays the cost difference versus an equivalent overhead system, under rule 25-6.078. That is why many newer Florida neighborhoods never had poles. It does not, by itself, pay to rebuild an existing overhead road.

Bundling with other construction

Paying once to open a road, instead of once for paving and again for conduit, can lower the combined public cost. It only works when the schedules match. See the phased approach.

Study first. Funding decision later. A feasibility study is not a tax, an assessment, a budget, or a choice about who pays.
