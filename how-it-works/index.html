How it works

Overhead wires move into conduit. The equipment does not disappear.

The wires move underground. The equipment does not disappear. Conduit replaces the span of wire. Transformers and switches move to boxes on pads, where crews can still reach them.

What residents should expect

Where a sentence below names FPL, it describes how one investor-owned utility explains neighborhood undergrounding. Those details are illustrative. They are relevant mainly where FPL serves, such as Miramar Beach, west of Scenic Highway 30A. They are not the default for 30A. CHELCO likely serves essentially all of Scenic Highway 30A and would set its own process if a corridor project proceeded on CHELCO lines. CHELCO also serves much of Santa Rosa Beach, Freeport, and inland Walton. These notes are not a CHELCO design and not a promise that either utility will build a project here.

Directional boring

A drill can pull conduit under a driveway or a stretch of pavement, with pits at the ends instead of a continuous cut. FPL says it uses boring on its neighborhood program to limit how much of a yard is opened. Rock, utilities already in the ground, and tight shoulders can rule it out on a given block.

Open trench

Where boring is not used, crews open the shoulder or the pavement, lay conduit, and restore the surface. Palm Beach’s town updates describe both drilling and ordinary street closures in the same program, with local access kept. A Walton feasibility study would have to say which method fits which block. Expect a mix.

Pad-mounted equipment

The transformer does not go in the conduit. It moves from the pole to a box on a concrete pad, with room to open the doors. On its projects, FPL describes a typical equipment easement of about 10 by 10 feet, and says the size can change with field conditions. A Walton design would set its own easements. The box stays visible.

Several utilities share the pole

Electric, cable, and phone often hang on the same pole. Undergrounding the power line does not move the others. The pole comes down only when the last attachment has a new home. If only the electric line moves, the pole can stay.

Service still switches at the house

Crews convert the line that runs to the meter. On FPL projects, customers may be asked to acknowledge a small junction box by the meter. Power is off for a short planned window during the switch, which is different from a storm outage.

Not invisible, and not outage-proof

Dig-ins, water, failed splices, and equipment problems can still take the lights out. Buried faults can take longer to find. The change is fewer outages from wind and trees on the spans that moved. On FPL’s program, the utility says it contacts customers before work and asks for an easement only where equipment has to sit.

Sources: FPL Storm Secure Underground Program (boring, easements, junction boxes, outreach); Town of Palm Beach project note, week of September 28, 2026 (drilling and local-access street closures).
